# Email in the PM's voice

## How the PM writes
- **Fewest words.** The standard is an attorney talking to a 12 year old: plain words, short sentences, no padding, and at most one line of pleasantry.
- **Lead with the ask or the answer.** Then give only the facts that support it: job name and number, dates, dollar amounts, model numbers.
- **One email, one purpose.** Number the items only when there are several asks.
- **No em dashes or en dashes.**
- **No stock phrases:** no "I hope this finds you well", "please don't hesitate", or "just wanted to".
- **Procurement sign-off:** "[PM Name], [PM Phone]". For the voice, read a few of the PM's own sent emails if you keep a sample.

## Process
1. Pull the facts from primary sources first: the email thread (outlook-read skill), the job folder, and your project management software. Every number and date must trace to a source. Never promise information the PM already sent.
   - **Warranty claim or RMA?** Before writing a word, read that product's actual warranty, IOM and seller terms, list what voids or limits it, and word every sentence to stay true without tripping it. Show the warranty source and the check with the draft (see the warranty rule in pm-house-rules).
2. Put the whole email in chat, ready to paste: To, Cc, Subject, body.
3. After every change, put the whole email in chat again.
4. Never send, and never save a draft, unless the PM asks for that exact action. Only the PM sends.

## Names and entity
- **New formal documents** use the company's full legal name with its trade name: [Company Legal Name] dba [Company DBA].
- **Documents under an existing contract** use the name on that contract.
- **Bank details:** never put account or routing numbers in an email body. Attach the bank letter instead.
- **Check names and roles before writing.** Use your own current list of who handles what (for example [Billing/AP], closeout status, entity and banking paperwork, install operations) and your vendor contacts list. Don't route from memory or an old email.
